FBX vs GLB: which 3D format is best for games?

FBX is Autodesk's proprietary 3D interchange format, the long-standing default for moving animated models between modelling tools and game engines. GLB is the binary form of glTF 2.0, an open standard from the Khronos Group built for delivering 3D at runtime, with the mesh, PBR materials, textures and animation packed into one file. The short answer: use GLB for Godot, the web, and sharing or archiving models, and FBX when your engine or animation pipeline expects it, which still often means Unity and Unreal character work.

The rest of this post goes through the differences that actually matter for game development.

FBX vs GLB at a glance

FBX GLB (glTF 2.0)
Owner Autodesk Khronos Group, open standard
Specification Not public, read through Autodesk's SDK Public and royalty-free
Files One .fbx, textures often separate One .glb with textures embedded
Materials Older material model, PBR varies by exporter Metallic-roughness PBR built in
Units and axes Vary by exporting program Always metres, Y up
Animation Skeletal, blend shapes, very mature Skeletal, morph targets, multiple clips
Compression None standard Draco or meshopt meshes, KTX2 textures
Unity Native Through a package such as glTFast
Unreal Engine Native Built-in importer in Unreal Engine 5
Godot Native since Godot 4.3 Native, the recommended format
Web (three.js, Babylon.js) Limited The standard

Materials

This is the difference you are most likely to notice. glTF defines a single metallic-roughness PBR material model, so a GLB exported from Blender should look the same in Godot, three.js or any other glTF viewer. Base colour, metallic, roughness, normal, occlusion and emissive maps all have a defined place.

FBX predates modern PBR. Its core material properties come from older lighting models, and each program fills in PBR values its own way. That is why an FBX that looks right in one tool often arrives in an engine with flat colours, missing roughness, or textures that need reconnecting by hand.

If texture maps are your pain point, our explainer on what an ORM texture is covers how glTF packs occlusion, roughness and metallic into one image.

Scale and orientation

glTF fixes its units as metres and its up axis as Y. FBX stores units and axes as settings, and different programs choose differently. The classic symptom is a model that imports at 100 times its intended size, or lying on its back. Most engines have import options to correct this, but with GLB there is nothing to correct.

Animation

Both formats handle skeletal animation, blend shapes (called morph targets in glTF) and multiple animation clips in one file. FBX has decades of tooling behind it for complex rigs, and services such as Mixamo export FBX, so it remains the default in many character pipelines. glTF animation is solid for most game use, and exporters have caught up a lot.

File size

A GLB packs everything into one binary file, and glTF supports Draco and meshopt mesh compression and KTX2 GPU-compressed textures. Compressed, a GLB is often a fraction of the size of the equivalent FBX, which matters for web games, downloads and large libraries. Our free GLB compressor shrinks a GLB in the browser, and the mesh reducer cuts the polygon count.

Engine support

  • Unity imports FBX natively. GLB and glTF need a package such as Unity's glTFast, added through the Package Manager.
  • Unreal Engine imports FBX natively, and Unreal Engine 5 has a built-in glTF importer.
  • Godot treats glTF as its preferred format. Godot 4.3 and later also import FBX natively, while earlier Godot 4 versions needed the external FBX2glTF tool.
  • Blender imports and exports both.
  • Web engines such as three.js and Babylon.js are built around glTF.

When to use FBX

  • Your engine is Unity and you do not want to add a glTF package.
  • You are working with an existing Maya, 3ds Max or MotionBuilder pipeline, or a service such as Mixamo that outputs FBX.
  • The asset pack you bought only ships FBX. Many Asset Store packs do.

When to use GLB

  • You use Godot, or you are building for the web.
  • You want a model to look the same everywhere, with PBR materials that survive the trip.
  • You want one self-contained file to share, sell or archive.
  • File size matters, and you want mesh and texture compression.

How to convert FBX to GLB

The free route is Blender:

  1. File, Import, FBX, then check the model, its scale and its materials.
  2. Fix anything that came in wrong, usually texture connections or roughness values.
  3. File, Export, glTF 2.0, and choose glTF Binary (.glb) as the format.

Then open the result in our free glTF viewer or model inspector to check it looks the way you expect. For OBJ files, our OBJ to GLB converter does the job in the browser.
