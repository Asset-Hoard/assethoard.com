You already own the asset. Good luck finding it.

Godot is brilliant at the project in front of you. It has no idea about the dozen other places your assets live.

📦

It's in a zip somewhere

Downloads, a backup drive, a project from two game jams ago. The FileSystem dock only sees the project you've got open.

📄

A .tres is just text

Materials, SpriteFrames and TileSets are blank icons in Explorer and Finder. The only way to see one is to copy it into a project and open it.

🔗

Then the paths break

That material points at res://textures/moss_albedo.png. Your project doesn't have a textures folder. Cue the dependency errors and ten minutes of hunting.

So you rebuy the pack, or make do with something worse.

How it goes with AssetHoard

01

Bring every pack in

Point AssetHoard at your downloads, your old projects and that backup drive. It indexes the lot, looks inside zips without extracting them, and recognises Godot projects and addons by their project.godot and plugin.cfg.

Then it does the bit nothing else does. It reads every .tres, follows each res:// path and finds the files on disk, even when the pack's author flattened the folders before zipping it up. Godot 3 and Godot 4 resources both work.

02

See what you've actually got

AssetHoard files each resource under what it really is, then shows it to you. Materials render on a lit sphere. SpriteFrames play, with an animation picker. TileSets show the whole atlas with terrain and collision marked. Fonts render whatever you type.

Filter to Materials and your Godot materials are right there, next to every other material you own.

03

Put it in your game

Drag it in. The dependencies come along, and your files are never overwritten.

🖱️

Drag it into Godot

Drag a .tres out of AssetHoard and drop it at the root of your project. Every file it references lands at its original res:// path, so nothing needs relinking.

Key features

  • Works for any file Godot can open
  • Drag a bundle and shared files only arrive once
  • The .tres itself is never modified
04

Do it all again next project

Your library outlives every project. Tag the forest tileset you loved and in your next game it's a search away, not a dig through last year's folders. It all lives on your machine, works offline and runs on Windows, macOS and Linux.
